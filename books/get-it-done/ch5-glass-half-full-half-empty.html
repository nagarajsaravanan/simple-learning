Chapter 5: The Glass Half Full and Half Empty

"Should you look at how much you have already accomplished, or how much you still have left to do? The answer depends on where you are on the journey."
— Dr. Ayelet Fishbach

1. The Progress Dilemma: Look Back or Look Forward?

When you work on a big goal like clearing the Zoho technical rounds, you have two ways to evaluate your progress:

  1. "To-Date" Framing (Looking Back): "I have already solved 15 core recursion problems."
  2. "To-Go" Framing (Looking Forward): "I still have 10 system design concepts left to master."

Dr. Fishbach’s research revealed that neither framing is always best. Knowing when to switch between them is the secret to relentless motivation.

2. The Two Rules of Progress Framing

PHASE 1: THE BEGINNING (0% to 50% Complete)
👉 FOCUS ON "TO-DATE" (LOOK BACK AT WHAT YOU DID)
   Why: You need to prove to your brain that you are committed.
   Thought: "I showed up for 5 days straight. I am building serious momentum!"

PHASE 2: PAST THE HALFWAY MARK (51% to 100% Complete)
👉 FOCUS ON "TO-GO" (LOOK FORWARD AT WHAT'S LEFT)
   Why: The "Goal Gradient Effect" kicks in. When the finish line is near, humans sprint!
   Thought: "Only 3 more problems left to complete this week's sprint. Let's knock them out!"

3. The "Goal Gradient Effect" in Action

Psychologist Clark Hull discovered that animals run faster the closer they get to food. The same is true for human ambition:

  • When you feel far away from a goal, you move slowly.
  • When you can see the finish line right in front of you, an adrenaline surge pushes you forward.

How to Trigger the Goal Gradient in Zoho Prep:

Instead of treating your prep as one gigantic, infinite marathon:

  • Break your preparation into bite-sized 5-problem sets.
  • When you are on Problem #4 of 5, your brain says: "Just one more to complete the set!" That final push feels effortless and fast.

4. Key Takeaways

  1. At the start, look back: Count your small initial wins to build belief that you are committed.
  2. Past the halfway mark, look forward: Focus on how close the finish line is to trigger a natural sprint.
  3. Keep finish lines close: Break large study topics into sets of 3 to 5 problems to stay in a permanent state of momentum.
