Chapter 3: Incentives Matter

"Incentives are powerful, but they are tricky. If you incentivize the wrong thing, you will get the exact opposite of what you want."
— Dr. Ayelet Fishbach

1. How Incentives Work (And Why They Backfire)

In Chapter 3, Dr. Fishbach explores extrinsic incentives (external rewards like money, scores, certificates, or prizes).

While rewards can push you to start, they carry a hidden danger: you get exactly what you reward, not what you actually intended.

The Famous "Cobra Effect"

In colonial Delhi, the government wanted to reduce venomous cobras. They offered a cash bounty for every dead snake brought in.
What happened? People started breeding cobras in their basements to collect money! When the government ended the bounty, breeders released the snakes, leaving the city with more cobras than when they started.

2. The Cobra Effect in Coding & Zoho Prep

Software candidates accidentally create their own Cobra Effect every day:

The Flawed IncentiveWhat You Actually End Up DoingThe Disastrous Interview Result
"I will solve 10 LeetCode problems today."You pick easy problems, copy-paste answers from Google, and check the box.When the Zoho interviewer asks you to write the logic on a whiteboard, your mind goes blank.
"I will spend 4 hours sitting at my desk."You sit with your laptop open, but spend 3 hours checking Discord, Reddit, and phone notifications.You feel exhausted from "sitting", but built zero real skill.
"I want to memorize all 23 Design Patterns."You memorize class names and definitions from a book.In Round 3 (App Design), you over-engineer a simple Ticket Booking system with 15 useless classes.

3. How to Set "Smart Incentives"

To use incentives effectively without getting trapped by fake metrics:

1. Reward the Deep Process, Not the Superficial Count

  • ❌ Don't reward yourself for "Solving 5 questions."
  • ✅ Reward yourself for: "Explaining the recursive backtracking state tree out loud on paper without looking at any hints."

2. Make the Reward Immediate

Your brain cares much more about an immediate reward than a distant one.

  • After finishing a focused 90-minute raw problem-solving block, reward yourself immediately:
    • Take a relaxing 20-minute walk.
    • Enjoy your favorite tea or snack.
    • Watch an episode of your favorite show completely guilt-free.

4. Key Takeaways

  1. Be careful what you measure: If you measure problem counts, you will cheat yourself by copying solutions.
  2. Reward deep struggle: Give yourself credit when you sit with a hard puzzle for 20 minutes without checking the answer.
  3. Keep rewards immediate: A small, instant reward today is 10x more motivating than a distant promise months away.
