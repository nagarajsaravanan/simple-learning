Chapter 2: Put a Number on It

"A goal without a specific number is just a wish. Numbers turn vague hopes into concrete targets."
— Dr. Ayelet Fishbach

1. Why Vague Goals Fail

Saying "I want to get better at coding" or "I want to prepare for Zoho" sounds good, but your brain cannot execute on vague statements.

  • When a goal has no number, you never know when you are done.
  • You never feel the satisfaction of crossing the finish line.
  • You end up working aimlessly, feeling tired, and making zero measurable progress.

2. The 3 Rules of Numbered Goals

Dr. Fishbach’s research highlights three essential rules for setting effective targets:

Rule 1: The "Goldilocks" Difficulty (Not Too Hard, Not Too Easy)

  • ❌ Too Easy: "Write 1 for-loop today." (Boring. No dopamine. No skill improvement).
  • ❌ Too Hard: "Solve 20 hard recursive backtracking problems today." (Terrifying. You will panic and end up on YouTube).
  • ✅ Just Right: "Solve 2 medium matrix problems from scratch on paper without looking at answers." (Challenging enough to stretch your brain, but achievable).

Rule 2: Use Target Ranges Instead of Rigid Numbers

Instead of setting a single rigid number, set a mini-range:

  • ❌ Rigid: "I must solve exactly 5 questions every single day."
    If life happens and you only solve 3, you feel like a failure and want to give up.
  • ✅ Range: "I will solve 2 to 4 questions today."
    • On busy/exhausting days: You hit 2 and feel victorious.
    • On high-energy days: You push to 4 and feel unstoppable.
    • Science Fact: People stick to ranges over 50% longer than single rigid targets.

Rule 3: Measure Outputs (Work Done), Not Just Inputs (Hours Sat)

  • ❌ Input Goal: "Sit at my desk for 3 hours."
    You will sit there, open 15 browser tabs, browse Reddit, and tell yourself you "studied."
  • ✅ Output Goal: "Implement the Railway Ticket Cancellation logic with 3 test cases."
    Once the code runs and passes edge cases, you are done. It drives intense, high-efficiency focus.

3. How to Apply This to Your Zoho Preparation

Here is how you put real numbers on your Zoho journey:

Preparation Stage❌ Vague (Will Fail)✅ Numbered & Concrete (Will Win)
Basic Logic (Round 1)"Practice loops and arrays.""Implement 5 array manipulations (e.g. rotate, remove duplicates, frequency counter) without built-in library helpers."
Advanced Coding (Round 2)"Do some recursion questions.""Solve 2 classic Zoho problems (e.g. Sudoku solver, Snake matrix traversal) completely on paper before typing."
System Design (Round 3)"Learn Object-Oriented Design.""Design 1 full CLI application (e.g. Call Center Routing System) with 4 clean classes and 0 global variables."

4. Key Takeaways

  1. Vague goals breed laziness: Numbers create healthy urgency and clear finish lines.
  2. Ranges beat rigid numbers: "2 to 4 problems" keeps your streak alive when life gets busy.
  3. Count the victory, not the clock: Finish the feature, don't just watch the clock tick.
