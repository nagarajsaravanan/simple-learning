2. 5-Minute Executive Summary

Most productivity advice focuses on sheer willpower, rigid discipline, or superficial time-blocking hacks. Dr. Ayelet Fishbach approaches goal achievement through decades of controlled behavioral research, revealing that motivation is a scientific design problem:

3. The 12-Chapter Roadmap

Part I: Choose Your Goal

  • Chapter 1: Goals Aren't Chores
    Why your brain hates chores, thinking big vs. thinking small, playing to win, and ditching fake preparation.
  • Chapter 2: Put a Number on It
    The Goldilocks rule, why ranges (2–4 problems) beat rigid targets, and measuring outputs instead of hours sat.
  • Chapter 3: Incentives Matter
    The Cobra Effect (perverse incentives), why measuring problem counts backfires, and smart rewards.
  • Chapter 4: Intrinsic Motivation
    Why immediate joy predicts long-term persistence, the hacker mindset, and temptation bundling.

Part II: Keep the Momentum

  • Chapter 5: The Glass Half Full & Half Empty
    When to look back (to build commitment) vs. when to look forward (to trigger the goal gradient sprint).
  • Chapter 6: The Middle Problem
    Why everyone slacks in the middle, how standards drop, and using 7-day sprints to crush the slump.
  • Chapter 7: Learning from Negative Feedback
    Overcoming ego defense mechanisms, calm live-interview debugging, and the 1-line post-mortem rule.

Part III: Juggling Goals (Self-Control & Patience)

  • Chapter 8: Goal Juggling
    Balancing multiple priorities vs. seasonal highlighting, and multi-finality wins.
  • Chapter 9: Self-Control
    Why iron willpower is a myth, friction engineering (phone in another room), and pre-commitments.
  • Chapter 10: Patience & Delayed Gratification
    Overcoming present bias, active waiting, and positive anticipation.

Part IV: The Social Side of Goals

  • Chapter 11: Pursuing Goals with Others
    Social contagion of ambition, explaining code out loud to peers, and avoiding the fake study group trap.
  • Chapter 12: Goals in Relationships & Teams
    The giving-advice paradox, shared goals, and alignment with Zoho's collaborative culture.
